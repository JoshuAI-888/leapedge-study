YouTube Intelligence · UX proposals for the 19 agreed features · 29 Sep 2026

YouTube Intelligence UX proposals

What each new feature changes in the interface, what else has to move to make room for it, and the states each screen needs. It is based on the code on main and on screenshots of the app running locally with its test data, on desktop and phone, in light and dark.

The overall shape

  • Four new pages: Daily report, Search, Trends and one page per channel. The sidebar is grouped into Read · Research · Sources · Your work · Operate.
  • Today becomes an inbox: today's report summary at the top, then what's new since your last visit, then the calls. A watchlist and the sentiment panel sit in the right-hand column.
  • The Analysis page leads with a verdict: a summary, numbered key points, then richer call cards. How the result was produced stays under Processing details.

Shared parts built once and reused

  • a split bar showing bullish / neutral / bearish
  • a sparkline
  • a row of headline-number tiles
  • a filter bar whose state lives in the page address
  • an Export menu
  • a step-by-step progress bar
  • an instrument label that also handles macro views
  • a trading-day date label
  • an unread dot
  • the quick-search box
Design skills: the "frontend-design" and "impeccable" skills you mentioned aren't installed in this session. I used the data-visualisation and page-design skills that are available, the app's own Finradar design tokens, and the spec's front-end section (§7). The chart colours below were run through the palette validator for colour-blind safety, not chosen by eye.

The app records seven stances (long, short, neutral, avoid, watch, hold, conditional). No chart can tell seven apart by colour. Charts and split bars therefore use the three sentiments the app already derives from stance (contracts.ts:126): bullish, neutral and bearish. Stance chips keep their text labels. Page structure

  • Header:
    • The US session date with previous/next arrows and a date picker that skips non-trading days.
    • A one-sentence consensus headline and the sentiment split bar.
    • Source count, check status and generation time. Cost stays in diagnostics.
  • Themes: 3–5 sections from the synthesis. Each bullet carries citations as chips (channel ▶ timestamp) that open the analysis at that moment. The briefing pipeline already requires a run or claim reference for every point.
  • In focus: ranked instruments, including macro rows (gap 13), with:
    • creator count
    • split bar
    • change against the previous session
    • one-line reason
    A row expands to show its source calls.
  • Where creators disagree: a new section that LeapEdge doesn't have. It lists instruments with calls on both sides and shows the two sets of quotes side by side. For an investment team, disagreement is often the most useful signal.
  • Video briefs (moved from Today, D3) and Sources: every analysis included, each with its verdict line (gap 5).
  • Archive: a right-hand column (desktop) or a list page (phone) of past sessions. Each shows its headline and split bar, so a week of mood reads at a glance.

States

  • Before 08:30 ET: "Today's report is generated at 08:30 ET (9:30 pm your time). Showing Fri 25 Sep."
  • Fewer than 3 videos: the report is not synthesised. The page shows the calls table and says why.
  • The check flagged points: an amber strip "2 points removed by the source check", with the removed points listed in a disclosure. They are never shown silently.
  • Generation failed: the error in plain words, with a Retry button (gap 12's retry pattern).

What else changes

  • Today: the summary card replaces "Material developments" at the top, and the "Across creators" panel is folded into In focus.
  • The public share page (/share/[token]) keeps working. The report page links to the existing share action only if you want it; sharing is out of scope, so no new share UI is added.
  • Methodology gets a "How the daily report is built" entry.

Phone

  • Single column. In focus becomes cards (instrument, split bar, reason). Previous/next becomes swipeable arrows in the header.

Design choices

  • Two result tabs, Calls and Videos, over the same filters. An analyst asks "what was said" (calls) as often as "what did they cover" (videos).
  • Every filter shows a live count for the current set. Options with a count of zero stay visible but greyed, so nothing disappears unexplained.
  • Active filters show as chips in the top row, with "Clear all". The whole state lives in the page address.
  • Text search matches anywhere in the title, thesis, quote and translation, including Chinese text. This fixes LeapEdge's prefix-only title search, which missed "…｜Tom Lee" and returned 0 results for "Stock".
  • Paging is "Load 50 more" with an honest total. There is no silent cap at 60.
  • The summary column recomputes for the filtered set: sentiment split, conviction spread, top instruments, top channels, and a preview of the hovered row.
  • Trust filter defaults to Text-checked, and the "N hidden by trust" line is always shown.

States

  • No results: suggests the one filter whose removal returns the most rows, e.g. "Remove 'Last 7 days' to see 12 calls".
  • Loading: results keep their previous render at reduced opacity. No skeleton flash.
  • Opened with no filters: shows the newest calls plus suggested entry points (top instruments this week).

What else changes

  • Today's "Find a call" box becomes a link into Search carrying the typed text.
  • Every instrument label and channel name in the app links to Search pre-filtered to it.
  • The Leaderboard row and the channel page get "See all calls →".

Phone

  • A "Filters (3)" button opens a full-height sheet with a sticky "Show 41 calls" button. The summary collapses into one line at the top ("41 calls · 33 ▲ 5 ● 3 ▼").

Why this chart and not LeapEdge's scatter

  • LeapEdge plots every call as a dot in four direction lanes. That shows volume badly: 40 overlapping dots read as one smear.
  • Weekly columns (bullish above the line, bearish below) show whether the balance is shifting. That is the question this page answers.
  • In the ticker view, a second chart below shares the date axis and puts each call on the price line. Calls against price is the view an investment team most wants, and LeapEdge doesn't have it. Two charts with separate scales avoid the misleading double axis.
  • In the channel and sentiment views the price chart is replaced by a call-dot timeline, with at most three lanes (bullish, neutral, bearish). That stays within the three-colour limit for scatter charts.

Interaction and states

  • Hover: a vertical crosshair snaps to the week, and the tooltip lists bullish, neutral and bearish counts. Each dot has a hit area of at least 24 px.
  • Click a column to filter the list below to that week. Click a dot to open the call.
  • Table view: a "Chart · Table" toggle shows the same data as rows, for accessibility and for export.
  • Few calls: with fewer than 5 calls in the range, the columns hide and the call list is shown directly, with a note saying so.
  • No prices for the instrument (macro views, most crypto without a price source): the price chart hides with "No price series for MACRO · Rates".
  • What else changes: the Daily report archive covers LeapEdge's "Chronicle", so Trends doesn't repeat it. The channel page (gap 4) reuses this chart with the channel preset.

Card layout

  • Line 1: instrument, stance, conviction and trust. Horizon and expiry are right-aligned, with the days remaining. When expiry is near or passed it turns amber, then shows as "expired".
  • Line 2: thesis. Line 3: the action, in the creator's terms.

  • Prompt and schema version bump: older calls show only the fields they have, with no empty "Catalysts: —" rows.

Design

Design

GAP 15

24-hour trending

Sentiment shift panel on Today

Catches names that suddenly get attention overnight, before a 7-day window would show them.

  • Honest labelling: the group header's hover text says "Tracked on this browser". The app has one shared passcode, so it can't know who has read what. Don't word it as if it were per person.
  • What counts as seen: opening the analysis. Scrolling past it on Today doesn't count.
  • Storage: the browser's local storage holds the seen IDs and the last-visit time. If storage is blocked, the page still works and shows no dots.

GAP 13

GAP 14

US trading day and team time zone

Every date · Settings · date pickers

Lines creator sentiment up with the market session it can move, instead of splitting one US session across two New Zealand calendar days.

  • Dates by session: every grouping and "day" label is by US session, "Mon 28 Sep · US session". Times inside a day show in the team's zone, e.g. "18:42 NZDT".
  • Hover explains the assignment: "Published Sat 26 Sep 14:00 NZDT → counted in Mon 28 Sep session (weekend)". The explanation matters more than the rule.
  • Date pickers and previous/next skip weekends and US market holidays. Holidays show as struck-through in the report archive with the holiday's name.
  • Market status in the header: a small line next to the page title reads "US market: pre-market · opens in 2 h 10 m". It tells a New Zealand team at a glance which session "today" means.
  • Settings: the team time zone is a searchable IANA select in the team section, and the digest time zone field is merged into it. Raw ISO timestamps are removed from the UI, starting with the Leaderboard's "As of".
GAP 10

Export

Search · channel page · Daily report · Saved · Leaderboard (existing)

Lets analysts join creator sentiment with their own models, positions and records in Excel or Python.

  • One Export menu ("Export ▾ → CSV · JSON") at the right of each page's filter row, always stating its scope: "Exports 41 calls matching these filters".
  • Columns are the same everywhere:
    • call id, session date, published at (UTC), channel, video title and link with timestamp
    • instrument and type, stance, sentiment, conviction, trust level
    • thesis, action, levels (original text and parsed value), catalysts, risks, horizon, expiry
    • original quote, translation
    JSON nests evidence spans. The Methodology page documents the columns.
  • The daily report exports as JSON (themes with citations, In focus rows) and as a calls CSV.
  • Large exports run on the server with paging. Past 5,000 rows the menu says "Preparing export…" and then downloads. The button is disabled when there are no rows, which fixes the Leaderboard's always-enabled button.
  • A footer line in every file states the filters, the trust basis, and "Trust describes the evidence, not investment quality". Exported numbers never pass as verified accuracy.
